**Phase:** A (understand) — in progress  
**Phase B:** —

## 1. What he does

He looks at the project’s public social presence (mainly Twitter / X, Spaces, and related content) and judges whether it is a **ghost**: activity that looks like a project, but without real organic interest.

He treats that judgment as part of the **same-operator / same-playbook** profile, alongside price band (~$0.3) and FDV (~$300M).

## 2. What he thinks it means

A genuine community is not present. The social layer is **stage dressing** — enough presence to look listable and fundable (VCs, CEX perps), not enough genuine demand to explain later OI, volume, or price.

The contrast he cares about: **VC logos + listings** on one side, **empty or fake socials** on the other. That mismatch is the tell.

## 3. What would have to be true

For this step to be a real process (not only taste):

1. There is a repeatable gap between **presented social activity** and **actual interested humans**.
2. That gap is common to the names he groups as golden crime, and less common (or shaped differently) on ordinary mid-cap alts.
3. “Botted” and “random people doing content” are not metaphors only — they point at specific failure modes of organic community.

The document **does not** say how he measures any of that. The method is tacit.

## Question 1 — How do we know socials are botted and it is a ghost project?

**Short answer from the document:** we don’t. He asserts it as something you can see. He does not give a procedure.

What “ghost” seems to mean in his usage (reconstructed, not quoted):

- **Ghost ≠ no socials.** These projects have accounts, posts, Spaces, and content.
- **Ghost = no real demand behind the activity.** The social layer does not look like people who care about the product or token for its own sake.

“Botted” in crypto-Twitter slang usually bundles several different things. The PDF does not separate them. Useful to split so we do not smuggle in a method he did not state:

| Possible reading of “botted” | What that would mean | Observable in principle? |
|------------------------------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| Fake followers | Bought / farm followers | Follower quality tools, age, geo, follow graphs |
| Fake engagement | Like/RT/reply pods or bots | Engagement rate vs follower count; reply quality |
| Scheduled empty posting | Cadence without conversation | Thread structure, reply depth, unique posters |
| Paid / amp networks | Same amp accounts across “unrelated” coins | Cross-coin account overlap |
| No organic discovery | Mentions only from the project or its contractors | Who initiates talk; search/mention graph |

His neighboring phrases — “no one is actually interested” and “random people” doing Spaces — suggest he is not only saying “bot score is high.” He is saying the **human activity that does exist still fails a authenticity / stake test**.

So a fair Phase A statement:

> “Botted / ghost” in this playbook is an **expert gestalt** over social emptiness + inauthentic amplification, used to flag candidates that already fit price/FDV. The PDF gives the label, not the test.

We should not pretend the document contains a detection algorithm. Any later Phase B work would be inventing or interviewing for that test — not extracting it.

## Question 2 — What does “random people” doing content mean?

**Short answer:** the PDF never defines “random.” We have to unpack the word against the rest of his claim.

In context, “random” is almost certainly **not** “unknown to me personally.” It is a claim about **relationship to the project**.

Plausible meanings, from strongest fit to weaker:

1. **No stake in the project**  
   Hosts and posters do not look like founders, core team, long-time community, or domain experts tied to the product. They look hired, rotated, or opportunistic.

2. **Interchangeable faces**  
   The people could be swapped for other paid hosts and nothing about the project’s story would change. Content is generic crypto-promo, not project-specific depth.

3. **No career or reputation continuity**  
   Accounts that appear for Spaces / shill threads and have no prior trail in that niche — or a trail of doing the same for many unrelated tokens.

4. **Mismatch with the VC raise**  
   Serious capital on the cap table, but the public voice of the project is temp labor and amp accounts. “Random” = not the caliber of presence you’d expect if the raise were for a real product with a real team communicating.

What “random” is probably **not** (unless he says so later):

- Literally strangers with zero followers.
- Only non-English or offshore creators.
- Anyone who is not a crypto-Twitter celebrity.

The important contrast in the sentence is:

> botted socials + random content people **yet** VC raises (including recurring names)

So “random” is doing work as the **human face of the ghost**: enough live content to look alive, not enough authentic team/community to look real. That is why it sits next to botting rather than being a separate thesis.
